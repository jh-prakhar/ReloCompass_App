# Employment Guide: Resume, Interviews, and Job Search

## Finding Employment Abroad as an International Student or Job Seeker

Whether you're looking for a part-time job while studying or a full-time position after graduation, understanding the local job market is essential.

### Resume / CV Guidance

Different countries have different resume conventions. Here's what to know:

#### General Principles
- Keep it to 1-2 pages (academic CVs can be longer)
- Tailor your resume to each job description
- Use action verbs: "developed," "managed," "analyzed," "created"
- Quantify achievements: "Increased sales by 20%" not just "Improved sales"
- Include keywords from the job posting (many companies use ATS filtering)
- Use a professional email address
- Proofread multiple times — typos can eliminate you from consideration

#### Country-Specific Resume Tips

**Canada**: 1-2 pages. Include a summary at the top. Canadian employers value volunteer experience. Do NOT include a photo, age, or marital status.

**Germany**: Lebenslauf format. Structured and formal. A professional photo is traditionally included. Education and work experience listed chronologically.

**UK**: 1-2 pages (called a CV). No photo. Include a personal profile at the top. Emphasize skills and achievements.

**Australia**: 2-3 pages. Include a career summary. Australian employers value a good cultural fit and soft skills. No photo required.

**USA**: 1 page preferred for entry-level. Called a resume, not a CV. No photo, age, or personal details. Results-focused.

### Interview Preparation

#### Common Interview Stages
1. **Phone/Video Screening**: 15-30 minutes, basic qualification questions
2. **Technical Interview**: Coding exercises, case studies, or skill assessments
3. **On-site Interview**: Multiple rounds with team members and managers
4. **Final Round**: Cultural fit, often with senior leadership

#### Interview Tips
- Research the company thoroughly: their products, values, recent news, competitors
- Prepare answers for common questions: "Tell me about yourself," "Why do you want to work here?", "What are your strengths and weaknesses?"
- Use the STAR method for behavioral questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result
- Prepare 3-5 thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer
- Dress appropriately for the company culture (research beforehand)
- Practice with mock interviews — your university career center can help
- Send a thank-you email within 24 hours of the interview

#### Virtual Interview Tips
- Test your camera, microphone, and internet connection before the interview
- Choose a quiet, well-lit space with a neutral background
- Dress professionally from head to toe (not just the top half)
- Look at the camera, not at your screen, when speaking
- Have a glass of water nearby
- Keep your resume and notes accessible but off-camera

### Part-Time Jobs for Students

Most student visas allow part-time work:
- **Canada**: Up to 24 hours/week off-campus during academic sessions
- **Germany**: 140 full days or 280 half days per year
- **UK**: Up to 20 hours/week during term time
- **Australia**: Up to 48 hours per fortnight (every two weeks)
- **USA**: On-campus only during the first academic year, then limited off-campus options

Popular part-time jobs for international students:
- Retail and hospitality (cafes, restaurants, shops)
- On-campus jobs (library, admin, tutoring, research assistant)
- Freelance work (check visa restrictions)
- Internships (often required by degree programs)

### Full-Time Job Search for Graduates

If you're seeking full-time employment with visa sponsorship:

1. **Use the right platforms**: LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, and country-specific job boards
2. **Target visa-sponsoring companies**: Research companies known for hiring international talent
3. **Leverage your university's career services**: Alumni networks, job fairs, employer partnerships
4. **Consider post-study work visas**: Many countries offer post-graduation work permits
5. **Build your professional network**: Attend industry events, join professional associations
6. **Optimize your LinkedIn profile**: Many recruiters search LinkedIn for candidates

### Post-Study Work Visas (for reference — always verify with official sources)

- **Canada**: Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) — up to 3 years
- **Germany**: 18-month residence permit to find employment after graduation
- **UK**: Graduate Route — 2 years (3 years for PhD graduates)
- **Australia**: Temporary Graduate Visa (subclass 485) — 2-4 years
- **USA**: OPT (1 year, 3 years for STEM fields) and H-1B visa sponsorship

### Workplace Etiquette by Country

Understanding workplace culture helps you succeed in a new job:

- **Punctuality**: Arriving on time is valued everywhere, but expectations vary. In Germany, 5 minutes early is on time. In some cultures, a few minutes late is acceptable.
- **Communication style**: Direct (Germany, Netherlands) vs. indirect (UK, parts of Asia). Observe how colleagues communicate and adapt.
- **Hierarchy**: Some workplaces are flat (startups, tech companies); others have clear hierarchies (traditional corporate). Understand the reporting structure.
- **Lunch culture**: In some countries, lunch is 30 minutes at your desk. In others, it's a 1-2 hour social affair. Follow the local norm.
- **After-work socializing**: Team dinners, happy hours, and social events are common. Participation is usually optional but helps with team integration.
