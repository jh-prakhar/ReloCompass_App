# Student Relocation: Banking and Health Insurance

## Setting Up Banking Abroad

Opening a bank account in your destination country is essential for managing tuition payments, receiving part-time job income, and paying rent. Here's what you need to know:

### Documents Typically Required
- Passport and visa/permit
- University acceptance letter or enrollment proof
- Proof of address (accommodation lease or dormitory confirmation)
- Initial deposit (varies by bank and account type)
- Tax identification number (some countries require this)

### Banking Tips
- **Research before you arrive**: Compare account types, fees, and international transfer options
- **Notify your home bank**: Let them know you'll be using your card abroad to avoid blocks
- **Consider digital banks**: Many offer free international transfers and no monthly fees
- **Set up online banking immediately**: This is your primary way to manage money
- **Keep emergency cash**: Have some local currency for the first week
- **Understand currency exchange**: Use comparison tools to get the best exchange rates

### Popular Student-Friendly Banks by Country
- **Canada**: RBC, TD, Scotiabank (many have student accounts with no monthly fees)
- **Germany**: N26, DKB, Sparkasse (student accounts with free withdrawals)
- **UK**: HSBC, Barclays, Monzo (student accounts with overdrafts)
- **Australia**: Commonwealth Bank, ANZ, Westpac (student accounts with no monthly fees)
- **USA**: Chase, Bank of America (student checking accounts)

## Health Insurance for International Students

Health insurance is mandatory for international students in most countries. Do not skip this.

### Country-Specific Requirements

**Canada**: Provincial health coverage varies by province. Some provinces (e.g., British Columbia, Alberta) cover international students under public health plans. Others (e.g., Ontario) require private insurance (e.g., UHIP). Check your province's requirements.

**Germany**: Public health insurance (e.g., TK, AOK) is mandatory for students under 30. Costs approximately EUR 110-120/month. Students over 30 or those with private insurance must arrange alternative coverage.

**United Kingdom**: The Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) is paid as part of your visa application and provides access to the NHS. The cost is GBP 470 per year for students.

**Australia**: Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is mandatory for the duration of your visa. Arranged through approved providers like Bupa, Medibank, or Allianz.

**United States**: Universities typically require proof of health insurance. Many offer student health plans (USD 1,000-3,000/year). Alternatively, you can purchase private insurance that meets university requirements.

### Health Insurance Tips
- Understand what your plan covers (doctor visits, hospitalization, prescriptions, mental health)
- Know how to file claims and what documentation you need
- Keep digital copies of your insurance card
- Find out if there's a student health center on campus
- Some countries require you to pay upfront and claim reimbursement later
- Mental health support is often included — don't hesitate to use it
