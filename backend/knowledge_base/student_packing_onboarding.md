# Student Relocation: Packing Checklist and University Onboarding

## International Student Packing Checklist

This checklist covers the essentials you need to bring when relocating abroad for studies. Adapt it based on your destination's climate and your personal needs.

### Essential Documents (Carry in Hand Luggage)
- [ ] Passport (valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival)
- [ ] Student visa / study permit
- [ ] University acceptance letter
- [ ] Proof of funds / financial guarantee
- [ ] Health insurance documents
- [ ] Academic transcripts and certificates (original + copies)
- [ ] Passport-size photos (6-10 photos)
- [ ] Medical records and prescriptions
- [ ] International driving permit (if applicable)
- [ ] Emergency contact information

### Electronics
- [ ] Laptop and charger
- [ ] Phone (unlocked for local SIM) and charger
- [ ] Universal power adapter
- [ ] USB drives / external hard drive
- [ ] Headphones
- [ ] Power bank

### Clothing
- [ ] Weather-appropriate clothing (research the climate)
- [ ] Formal outfit for interviews and presentations
- [ ] Comfortable walking shoes
- [ ] Traditional clothing for cultural events
- [ ] Rain jacket / umbrella / winter coat (as needed)

### Personal Items
- [ ] Prescription medications (enough for first 2-3 months)
- [ ] Glasses / contact lenses (with spare)
- [ ] Toiletries (travel-size for the first week)
- [ ] First aid kit (basic supplies)

### Financial
- [ ] Local currency cash (enough for the first week)
- [ ] Credit/debit cards (notify bank of international use)
- [ ] International money transfer app installed

## University Onboarding Checklist

Once you arrive, complete these tasks in your first two weeks:

### Week 1
- [ ] Attend international student orientation
- [ ] Collect your student ID card
- [ ] Set up your university email and online portal
- [ ] Register for classes
- [ ] Open a local bank account
- [ ] Get a local SIM card / mobile plan
- [ ] Register with the university health center
- [ ] Explore campus facilities (library, gym, study spaces)

### Week 2
- [ ] Complete any required immigration registration (some countries require this)
- [ ] Join student societies and clubs
- [ ] Familiarize yourself with public transportation
- [ ] Set up your accommodation (furniture, internet, utilities)
- [ ] Meet your academic advisor
- [ ] Understand the grading system and academic expectations
- [ ] Find a part-time job if you plan to work (check visa restrictions on work hours)

### Cultural Adaptation Tips
- Attend cultural exchange events organized by the university
- Practice the local language (even basic phrases help)
- Be patient with yourself — culture shock is normal
- Connect with other international students from your home country
- Find cultural grocery stores and restaurants for familiar food
- Explore your new city on weekends
- Establish a routine that includes study, social, and self-care time
