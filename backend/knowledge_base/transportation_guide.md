# Transportation Guide for International Students

## Getting Around Your New City

Understanding local transportation is crucial for international students. This guide covers the main modes of transport available in popular student destinations.

### Public Transportation Overview

Most cities with universities have excellent public transit systems. Here's what to expect:

#### Metro / Subway Systems
- Fast and reliable for medium to long distances
- Available in major cities: Toronto (TTC), London (Tube), Berlin (U-Bahn), New York (Subway), Paris (Metro)
- Most operate from approximately 5:00 AM to 1:00 AM (some run 24/7 on weekends)
- Download the official transit app for real-time schedules

#### Buses
- Cover areas not served by metro/rail
- Usually the cheapest option
- May require exact fare or a transit card
- Night buses often available on limited routes

#### Trains / Light Rail
- Connect suburbs to city centers
- Useful for intercity travel (e.g., GO Transit in Toronto, S-Bahn in Berlin)
- Student discounts available on many national rail services

### Student Travel Cards and Discounts

Most transit systems offer discounted fares for students. Here are examples:

**Canada**:
- Toronto: TTC Post-Secondary Student Pass (requires valid student ID)
- Monthly pass: approximately CAD 128.15 (discounted from regular fare)
- PRESTO card for GO Transit and regional services

**Germany**:
- Deutschlandticket: EUR 49/month for nationwide public transport (regional trains, buses, metro)
- Some universities include a semester transit pass in student fees (Semesterticket)

**United Kingdom**:
- 16-25 Railcard or 26-30 Railcard: 1/3 off train fares
- Student Oyster Card (London): 30% discount on Travelcards
- Trainline app for cheap advance tickets

**Australia**:
- Concession fares for international students (varies by city and state)
- Opal card (Sydney), Myki (Melbourne), Go Card (Brisbane)
- Some states offer special international student concession cards

**United States**:
- Varies by city: NYC MetroCard, Chicago Ventra, SF Clipper
- Some universities offer free campus shuttles
- International Student Identity Card (ISIC) for various discounts

### Airport Transfers

Getting from the airport to your accommodation safely and affordably:

- **Public transit**: Most airports are connected to the city via metro or express train. This is usually the cheapest option.
- **Airport shuttle**: Shared van services that drop off at major locations
- **Taxi/ride-share**: Uber, Lyft, or local taxi services. More expensive but convenient with luggage
- **University pickup**: Some universities offer free airport pickup for new international students at the start of each semester. Check with your university's international office.

### Ride-Sharing Apps
- Uber and Lyft are available in most major cities
- Bolt and Free Now are popular in Europe
- Ola operates in Australia, UK, and New Zealand
- Always verify the license plate before getting in
- Share your trip status with a friend

### Bicycle Systems
- Many cities have bike-share programs (e.g., Bike Share Toronto, Santander Cycles London, Call-a-Bike Berlin)
- Monthly or annual passes are cost-effective for regular users
- Always wear a helmet (legally required in some countries)
- Understand local cycling rules and bike lane markings
- Invest in a good bike lock — bike theft is common near campuses

### Transportation Tips
1. Download offline maps (Google Maps, Citymapper) before you arrive
2. Get a transit card as soon as possible
3. Learn the peak hours to avoid crowded transit
4. Keep emergency taxi numbers saved
5. Some cities offer free transit zones in the city center
6. Consider proximity to transit when choosing accommodation
