Transportation

Getting around your new city — official providers first.

Always confirm fares, passes and rules on the operator's official site — they change frequently and student discounts usually require enrollment proof from your university.

First month transport plan

  1. Before arrival: check your university's transport page for student pass programs.
  2. Week 1: get a local payment/travel card (e.g. PRESTO, Opus, Oyster, Suica) at an airport or station kiosk.
  3. Week 1–2: apply for the student/ concession fare once you have your enrollment letter.
  4. Week 2–3: map your daily commute (home ↔ campus/work) and compare monthly pass vs pay-per-ride.
  5. Ongoing: keep proof of student status with you when using concession fares.

Popular destinations — official operators

🇨🇦 Canada

Toronto (TTC), Montréal (STM), Vancouver (TransLink). PRESTO (Ontario) and Opus (Québec) are the main travel cards.

ttc.ca ↗ · stm.info ↗ · translink.ca ↗

🇦🇺 Australia

Opal (Sydney), myki (Melbourne), Go Card (Brisbane). International students get concession fares in most states.

transportnsw.info ↗ · ptv.vic.gov.au ↗

🇩🇪 Germany

Deutschland-Ticket — one monthly ticket for all regional transport nationwide. Most universities also offer a semester ticket.

bahn.com ↗ · deutschland-ticket.de ↗

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

Oyster / contactless in London, railcards for long-distance discounts, and 16–25 or student railcards.

tfl.gov.uk ↗ · nationalrail.co.uk ↗

🇺🇸 United States

Systems vary by city — MTA (NYC), CTA (Chicago), Clipper (SF). Many universities include transit passes in fees.

mta.info ↗ · transitchicago.com ↗

✈️ Airport transfers

Prefer official airport express trains/buses over unlicensed taxis. Most major airports publish fare ranges on their official sites.
